1The idea & the metric

Standard accuracy treats every mistake as equally bad, yet a clear-cut error (annotators agree 10–0) matters far more than an ambiguous borderline one. NEC weights each error by a per-example cost |Δ|.

Normalized Excess Cost (Eq. 1) $$\mathrm{NEC}=\frac{\sum_i |\Delta_i|\,\mathbf{1}[\hat y_i \neq \operatorname{sign}(\Delta_i)]}{\sum_i |\Delta_i|}$$
Uniform costs ⇒ NEC = error rate. A high error / NEC ratio means mistakes fall on low-cost, ambiguous cases.

4Claim 3 — three cost sources

Vote-margin and threshold-distance derivations run on public data. For Jigsaw, vote counts are reconstructed from aggregate toxicity and annotator-count fields.

Eq. 3 — multi-annotator votes$$\Delta_i=\log\frac{n_{\text{yes}}+1}{n_{\text{no}}+1}$$
Eq. 4 — threshold distance$$\Delta_i=z_i-\tau$$

Third source: direct ratings (Δ = score − midpoint) — synthetic demo; iNat ratings unreleased. Real Fig. 2 panels reproduce.

Red line marks the Δ=0 decision boundary; the shape of each cost distribution drives the size of its error/NEC gap.

8 Takeaways
Idea.Weight errors by disagreement-derived cost |Δ|; NEC exposes model quality error rate hides.
Result.NEC ≪ error rate reproduces on text, image & tabular (2.9×, 1.7×, 1.4×).
Training.Cost-weighting is inconsistent as claimed — helps Turkey, harms Jigsaw; mechanism unconfirmed.
Honesty.iNaturalist data-blocked; fine-tuning blocked, no GPU credits (402) — reported, not proxied.
